**KPI (Key Performance Indicator):** a measurable value that shows how well a part of the business is meeting its goals.

Starbucks and Best Buy sell different things, but both **sell** a product, **market** it, **run operations** to deliver it, **manage employees**, and **track money**. Those are the five components used below.

---

## 1. Sales

| KPI | What it measures | Formula |
|-----|------------------|---------|
| Revenue growth rate | How fast sales are increasing | (Current revenue − Prior revenue) ÷ Prior revenue × 100 |
| Average order value (AOV) | Average spend per transaction | Total revenue ÷ Number of orders |
| Sales conversion rate | Share of leads/visitors who buy | Purchases ÷ Leads (or visitors) × 100 |
| Sales target attainment | Performance against the sales goal | Actual sales ÷ Sales target × 100 |
| Customer retention rate | Share of customers who keep buying | (Customers at end − New customers) ÷ Customers at start × 100 |

## 2. Marketing

| KPI | What it measures | Formula |
|-----|------------------|---------|
| Customer acquisition cost (CAC) | Cost to win one new customer | Sales & marketing spend ÷ New customers |
| Return on marketing investment (ROMI) | Revenue earned per marketing dollar | (Revenue from marketing − Marketing cost) ÷ Marketing cost × 100 |
| Customer lifetime value (CLV) | Total revenue expected from one customer | Avg. purchase value × Purchase frequency × Customer lifespan |
| Campaign conversion rate | How well a campaign turns viewers into actions | Conversions ÷ Clicks (or visitors) × 100 |
| Cost per lead (CPL) | Cost to generate one potential customer | Campaign spend ÷ Leads generated |

## 3. Finance

| KPI | What it measures | Formula |
|-----|------------------|---------|
| Gross profit margin | Profit left after the direct cost of goods | (Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue × 100 |
| Net profit margin | Profit left after all expenses | Net income ÷ Revenue × 100 |
| Operating cash flow | Cash generated by day-to-day operations | Net income + Non-cash expenses ± Working-capital changes |
| Current ratio | Ability to pay short-term bills | Current assets ÷ Current liabilities |
| Budget variance | How far actual spending is from plan | (Actual − Budget) ÷ Budget × 100 |

## 4. Human Resources

| KPI | What it measures | Formula |
|-----|------------------|---------|
| Employee turnover rate | How many employees leave | Employees who left ÷ Average headcount × 100 |
| Time to hire | Speed of filling an open role | Avg. days from job posting to offer accepted |
| Cost per hire | Cost of recruiting one employee | Total recruiting cost ÷ Number of hires |
| Absenteeism rate | Unplanned time off | Days absent ÷ Scheduled workdays × 100 |
| Employee engagement (eNPS) | How likely employees are to recommend the company as a workplace | % Promoters (9–10) − % Detractors (0–6) |

## 5. Operations

| KPI | What it measures | Formula |
|-----|------------------|---------|
| Inventory turnover | How quickly stock is sold and replaced | COGS ÷ Average inventory |
| Order fulfillment cycle time | Time from order to delivery | Avg. (Delivery time − Order time) |
| On-time delivery rate | Reliability of delivery promises | On-time orders ÷ Total orders × 100 |
| Capacity utilization | How much available capacity is used | Actual output ÷ Maximum possible output × 100 |
| Defect / error rate | Quality of products or orders | Defective units (or orders) ÷ Total units × 100 |
